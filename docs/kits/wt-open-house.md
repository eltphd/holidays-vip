# Open House — draft 1 (for audit round 1)

**Category:** Whole Table · **Format:** make · **Kit id:** `wt-open-house` · **Price:** $29 digital · **Files by:** Dec 1
**Who it's for:** the person hosting, and the person who gives it to the person hosting.
**One line:** An affirming Kwanzaa or winter-break gathering, without a speech.

**Owners for audit:** trans auditors (name tags, pronouns, discreet mode) · married gay men (the welcome, the scripts) · adult (host run-of-show).

---

## Before you open this (read this first)

This kit doesn't ask anyone to come out, be outed, or explain themselves. Not the host, not a guest, not the cousin nobody's sure about. The whole thing is built so that everybody who's coming is already welcome, and nobody has to say so.

It shipped to you in discreet mode. The email said "Your holidayz.vip kit." The file is named `holidayz-winter-kit-01.pdf`. The cover reads as a winter kit. If you're a teen or an adult in a house that isn't safe yet, you can own this.

**What this asks of you**
- Time: about 90 minutes of prep across the week, and the gathering itself.
- Sensory: candles (optional; battery candles work), music at a volume you set, one loud moment (the toast), no required touch.
- Low-demand version: skip to "The bare minimum" at the end. It's a real version, not a consolation.

---

## 1. The welcome ("everyone's name, everyone's person")

The welcome is 40 seconds. It happens once, when most people are in the room. It does not single anyone out.

The host says something like this, in their own words:

> "Before we eat. Everybody here has a name, and everybody here has people. Some of you brought your person, some of you are somebody's person, some of you came alone and you're still somebody's. Say your name when you get to the table, and say who you came with if you want to. That's it. That's the rule in this house: anyone can pass."

That's the whole speech. What it does: it makes "who you came with" ordinary. Nobody is introduced as anything. Nobody has to correct anybody. The person who came with their partner says "I'm Marcus, this is Devin," and the sentence is exactly as long as the one their aunt said.

**Variations**
- If the host isn't a talker: put the sentence on the first table card instead (card 1, below) and read it out.
- If a relative is going to try to run the room: the host says the welcome before that relative arrives, or hands the welcome to the teen. Teens can say "anyone can pass" with more authority than anyone.

---

## 2. Place cards and name tags (pronoun-optional)

The templates are the same for everybody, which is the point. Two lines on each card:

```
  ______________________
  ( ____________________ )
```

Line one is the name. Line two is in parentheses and it's optional. A guest can write pronouns there, or a nickname, or what they want to be called tonight, or nothing. The host fills in line one for everybody ahead of time and leaves line two blank for everybody. Nobody's card looks different.

**Why parentheses and not "pronouns:"** a printed label that says "pronouns" turns the second line into a test. A blank line in parentheses is an invitation. Some people will write "she/her," some will write "Auntie," some will write "the one who brought the mac and cheese." All of those are correct answers.

**Do not** pre-fill pronouns for anyone, including people whose pronouns you know. It's theirs to write.

**Print sheet:** eight cards per page, cardstock if you have it, plain paper if you don't. Fold on the dotted line. (Layout in the PDF; the template uses a plain serif and no decoration on the second line.)

## 3. Host run-of-show

This is for a gathering of six to twenty people, afternoon into evening. Shift the times to your day. Nothing here is required in this order.

| When | What | Who |
|---|---|---|
| Day before | Cards filled in (line one only). Playlist queued. "Who's not at the table" note read (section 5). | Host |
| 1 hour before | One person you trust knows the three scripts (section 5). Candles or lights set. | Host + one |
| Arrivals | Music on. Cards at the door, not at seats, so people find their own. | Whoever's by the door |
| Most people in the room | The welcome (section 1). Forty seconds. | Host, or the teen |
| Table | Card 1 read out. Eat. | Anyone |
| Between courses, or not at all | One table card per stretch of the evening (section 6). Skip any. | Whoever picks it up |
| When it's right | The toast: "To everybody who's here, and everybody who's ours." Glass up, that's all. | Host |
| Before the first person leaves | Card 7. Photo if people want one; nobody is in a photo who doesn't want to be. | Anyone |
| After | Host sits down. | Host |

**Leaving early is built in.** If anyone needs to go, they go. The host doesn't make a thing of it. (The full "leave early with love" plan lives in Coming Home; this kit just makes sure the door is open.)

## 4. Playlist and menu cards

**Playlist card.** Blank on purpose, with four prompts down the side:
- One song that was playing in the kitchen when you were small
- One song your person loves that you pretend not to
- One song for the cousin who's not here
- One song that clears the room, for the end

The host asks three or four people for one song each, ahead of time. That's the playlist. It's short and it's everybody's.

**Menu card.** One card, the dishes and who made them, first names only. If a dish came from someone who's passed, their name goes on it: "Big Mama's greens (by Renee)." This is the only place in the kit where a person is named without being asked, and it's a person who isn't in the room.

---

## 5. The "who's not at the table" note, and three scripts

Read this before the day. It's for the host and one other person.

**The note.** Somebody isn't at this table. Maybe they weren't invited because of who they love. Maybe they didn't come because they weren't sure they could bring who they love. Maybe they're in the room and not sure the room knows. The welcome (section 1) is for that person. You don't need to name them. You need to have thought about them before you set the table, so the table is already set for them.

**The scripts.** Somebody may say something. An uncle, a grandmother, a guest who didn't read the room. These are three things the host or the trusted person can say. They're short because long is a debate, and a debate is exactly what the person who was just talked about doesn't want to sit through.

**Script one, the redirect (first offense, low heat):**
> "Not tonight. Pass the yams."

Said warmly, said once, then the conversation moves. Most of the time that's enough. It doesn't argue; it declines.

**Script two, the boundary (it continues, or it was aimed at someone):**
> "In this house everybody who's here is welcome. That includes you. Let's leave it there."

Said to the relative, not about them. "That includes you" is doing the work: it keeps them at the table while ending the subject.

**Script three, the exit (it's not stopping):**
> "I'm going to step out with [name] for a minute. Y'all keep eating."

The host or trusted person leaves the room with the person who was targeted, or with the relative, whichever is right. Not a scene. The table keeps eating. The evening is not ruined; one conversation is over.

**What the scripts don't do:** they don't explain, educate, or convince. That's not the host's job at their own table on a holiday. Winning the argument isn't the goal. Everybody still being at the table is.

## 6. Seven table cards (the Nguzo Saba, read through community)

One card per stretch of the evening, or one per night if you're spreading this across Kwanzaa. Each card has the principle, one sentence on what it means here, a thing to do with your hands, and one question. Skip any card. Skip all of them and just eat; the kit still worked.

**Card 1 — Umoja (Unity)**
*What it means here:* Everybody who came is the family that came.
*Hands:* Everyone writes one word for tonight on the back of their place card. Cards go in the middle of the table.
*Question:* "Who taught you how to set a table?"

**Card 2 — Kujichagulia (Self-Determination)**
*What it means here:* You say who you are. Nobody says it for you.
*Hands:* Line two of your place card is yours. Fill it, change it, or leave it. (This is the only card that points at the name tags, and it points at everybody's.)
*Question:* "What's a name somebody calls you that you love?"

**Card 3 — Ujima (Collective Work and Responsibility)**
*What it means here:* The dishes are everybody's. So is the cousin who's having a hard year.
*Hands:* Clear the table together. No one person does it.
*Question:* "Who in this family shows up when it's not their turn?"

**Card 4 — Ujamaa (Cooperative Economics)**
*What it means here:* What we have, we have between us.
*Hands:* The menu card. Add who made what, and who taught them. Keep the card.
*Question:* "What's a recipe you'd pay money to have written down?"

**Card 5 — Nia (Purpose)**
*What it means here:* We gather on purpose, not by accident.
*Hands:* Light a candle (or turn one on) for the people who aren't here. Say a name if you want. Don't if you don't.
*Question:* "What's one thing you want the youngest person here to remember about tonight?"

**Card 6 — Kuumba (Creativity)**
*What it means here:* We make the holiday; it doesn't come in a box, even this one.
*Hands:* The teen (or the youngest adult) designs one thing for next year: a dish, a rule, a song, a seat. Write it on the card.
*Question:* "What did we do this year that we should keep?"

**Card 7 — Imani (Faith)**
*What it means here:* We believe in each other, including the ones we don't understand yet.
*Hands:* The toast: "To everybody who's here, and everybody who's ours." Then the photo, for the people who want one.
*Question:* None. Just the toast.

## 7. The bare minimum (low-demand version)

If you have twenty minutes and no energy, do these three and nothing else:

1. Fill in line one on the cards. Leave line two blank.
2. Say the welcome, or read card 1.
3. Know script one ("Not tonight. Pass the yams.").

That's an affirming table. Everything else in this kit is extra.

## Open questions for the auditors (round 1)

- Trans auditors: is the parentheses line the right mechanic, or does it still read as a test in some rooms? Would you want a third option (a small blank space with nothing around it)?
- Married gay men: does "everyone's name, everyone's person" land, or is "person" too cute? What word would you use at your own table?
- Adult: is the run-of-show too much structure for a real house? What would you cut?
- Everyone: the three scripts. Are these things you'd actually say? What would you say instead?
