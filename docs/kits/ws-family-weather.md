## 1. The weather report

*Root:* "How you doing?" "I'm blessed." "I'm making it." "Hanging in there." We've always had a way to say how we are without saying why. This just gives the house five words and one rule.

**The words.** Everybody uses the same five. A teen and a grandmother say the same thing.

- **Clear.** I'm good. Come talk to me.
- **Cloudy.** I'm here. I'm a little low. Nothing's wrong with you.
- **Windy.** I'm short today. If I snap, it's not about you. Give me a wide berth.
- **Fog.** I'm here but I'm slow. Don't hand me anything to do. Don't ask me to decide.
- **Storm.** Give me room. I'll come find you when it passes.

**When it's said.** Once, when most people are in the room and before food. The host starts: "Weather?" Everybody who wants to says their word. That's it. Around the room in fifteen seconds. Anyone can update later, any time, with two words: "Weather update: fog."

**The rule: nobody asks why.** Not "why," not "what happened," not "you okay?" not a look. The only reply to somebody's weather is "heard," or a nod, or nothing. A person's reasons are theirs. The weather is the only part the room gets.

**What it's for.** It tells people how to treat you for the next hour without you explaining yourself, and it lets the person who's "storm" be at the table anyway. It's not a mood chart. Nobody writes it down.

**Ways to say it without saying it:**
- A fridge card with everybody's name and a magnet. Move your magnet. (Template in the PDF: five columns, first names down the side.)
- Text your word to the host. The host doesn't announce it; the host just knows.
- Hold up fingers. One for clear, five for storm. Kids like this one. So do elders who won't say "storm" in front of the grandbabies.

- Time: 1 minute, once, and 5 seconds for any update.
- Sensory: talking, or a magnet, or a hand.
- Low-demand: say "pass." Or say nothing; not saying your weather is not the same as saying "clear," and nobody gets to read it that way.

## 2. Repair scripts

*Root:* "Come here and let me fix your collar." The Black family repair was never a sit-down. It's a plate handed over, a "you eat?", a ride offered. Short, sideways, and it doesn't start with a conversation about the thing. These four decline to argue.

**Script one, same day (it came out wrong):**
> "That came out wrong. I'm not going to explain it. It came out wrong."

Not "I'm sorry you took it that way." Not a reason. The reason is a second argument. This one closes the first.

**Script two, you still think you're right and it still went bad:**
> "I still think what I think. I didn't need to say it like that."

You don't have to change your mind to repair how you said it. This one lets both things be true and moves on.

**Script three, old stuff (it's been years):**
> "I'm not opening that today. I'm still sitting next to you."

For the cousin, the sibling, the parent where the thing is too big for a holiday. It doesn't pretend the thing is gone. It says today we're at the same table, and that's what today is.

**Script four, the one you send after:**
> "Today got away from me. I'm glad you came."

A text, that night or the next morning. It doesn't name what happened. Whoever gets it knows.

**What the scripts don't do:** explain, win, or ask the other person to agree. If the other person says "you should be sorry," the script is done and you don't add to it. A repair is something you put down, not something you negotiate.

- Time: 10 seconds each.
- Sensory: talking, or a text. Script four is fully silent.
- Low-demand: script four. Send it tonight.

## 3. The boundaries card (two sides)

The kit calls it a boundaries card because that's what it is. The card itself talks like a person. Print it, fold it: one side for the host, one for the guest. Both sides are true in the same house at the same time.

### Side A: the host

**Things I get to decide:**
- What time we eat and what time it ends.
- Who's invited.
- What's on the menu.
- What we don't talk about at my table.
- Whether the TV is on.

**Things I don't get to decide:**
- Who somebody brought. If they're at the table, they're a guest, and they get treated like one.
- What somebody eats or doesn't.
- Whether somebody leaves early. They get to go.
- What somebody says their weather is.

**A line I can say out loud:** "It's my house, so it's my table. Everybody at it is welcome, and that's not up for discussion tonight."

### Side B: the guest

**Things I get to decide:**
- Whether I come.
- When I leave.
- What I eat.
- What I answer and what I don't.
- Who I sit next to.

**Things I don't get to decide:**
- What's said at the host's table about the host's rules. It's their house.
- Whether the argument from last year comes through the door with me. It stays in the car.
- What somebody else's weather is.

**A line I can say out loud:** "I came because I wanted to be here. I'm going to eat and I'm going to leave when I leave, and both of those are fine."

**About "what we don't talk about at my table."** The host gets that one, and it cuts both ways. A host can rule out politics. A host can also rule out the relative's commentary about who somebody brought. That's what the rule's for.

- Time: 2 minutes to read.
- Sensory: none. It's a card.
- Low-demand: read the side that's yours. Skip the other side.

## 4. Same story, three versions

*Root:* every family has the story that gets told every year, and every year it gets told by the same person. The year the turkey caught fire. The year somebody didn't come. The year of the house. This exercise gives the story to three people instead of one.

**Pick the story.** One everybody knows. Old enough that it's a story, not a wound. If somebody's still bleeding from it, pick a different one. Any person in the room can veto a story without saying why: "Not that one." Done.

**Pick three people who were there.** Three different seats. The one who tells it every year, and two who usually don't get the floor.

**The turns.**
1. The person who usually doesn't get the floor goes first. Two minutes, timed on a phone. They tell the story the way it happened from where they were sitting.
2. Nobody corrects. Nobody adds. Nobody says "that's not what happened" or "you left out the part where." Not with words, not with a face. Everyone else is listening, and that's their whole job.
3. Second person. Same two minutes. Same rules.
4. Third person. Same.
5. When the third one finishes, somebody says: "Three people were in that room. Three things happened." And it's over. Nobody votes on which one was right.

**The pass.** Any of the three can say "pass" when it's their turn. They stay in the circle. Nobody fills their two minutes. The story has two versions that year and that's fine.

**What this isn't.** It's not a trial. Nobody leaves agreeing. Three people heard three versions out loud, once, without being corrected, and the story got bigger than one person's telling.

**If it gets hot.** The timer is the rule. When two minutes end, the turn ends. If somebody corrects, the host says "not yet," and the turn continues. Anybody can say "let's stop there," and you stop there. Nobody has to say why.

- Time: 20 minutes, including picking the story.
- Sensory: sitting in a circle, listening for a while. Voices. It's the most people-facing thing in the kit. Nobody has to look at the speaker; looking at the table is fine.
- Low-demand: be one of the listeners. Or pick a lighter story: the year the dog got the ham. Same rules, no weight.

## 5. Ending a conversation without ending the relationship

*Root:* "All right now." "Let me let you go." "Tell your mama I said hey." We already have a hundred ways to hang up the phone that mean *this is over and we're fine.* These are two for the table.

**Line one, when you're done with this subject:**
> "I'm done with this part. I'm not done with you."

**Line two, when you need to leave the whole thing where it is:**
> "I'm going to stop here. Call me Tuesday."

Say it and then do something with your hands. Refill your cup. Take a plate to the kitchen. The hands part is what tells the room it's actually over.

**The receiving line.** If somebody says one of these to you, the answer is:
> "Okay. Tuesday."

Not "so you're just going to walk away." Not "we're not finished." You're not finished; that's what Tuesday is. They left the conversation, not the relationship, and they told you where to find them.

**If you can't say it out loud:** stand up, take your plate to the kitchen, and don't come back to that seat. That's the same sentence in a different language, and everybody in the house speaks it.

**Tuesday has to be real.** If you say Tuesday, somebody calls Tuesday. It doesn't have to be about the thing. "Hey. Tuesday." That's enough. The second half is what makes the first half something a person can trust.

- Time: 5 seconds.
- Sensory: talking, or standing up.
- Low-demand: the standing-up version.

## The bare minimum (low-demand version)

If you have two minutes and no patience:

1. Say "Weather?" once before food, and don't ask anybody why.
2. Know line one: "I'm done with this part. I'm not done with you."
3. Put the "this isn't therapy" card on the fridge.

That's a house with weather. Everything else in this kit is extra.

## Open questions for the auditors (round 1)

- **Adult:** the five weather words. Are these the right five? Is "fog" a word your mother would say, or does it read like an app? What word is missing (the one for "I'm fine and I'm lying")?
- **Adult:** the old-story exercise. Would your family actually do this, or does it get shut down at "pick the story"? Is twenty minutes honest?
- **Adult:** "Tuesday has to be real." Is that asking too much of a house where nobody calls anybody, or is it the only part that makes the line trustworthy?
- **Lesbians:** the boundaries card, side B. You brought your wife to a table where one relative has opinions. Does "who somebody brought" on the host's don't-decide list do enough, or does the guest side need its own line for the person who came with you? What would it say?
- **Lesbians:** the repair scripts. Script three ("I'm not opening that today. I'm still sitting next to you.") is written for the relative who hasn't accepted your marriage. Is that a script you'd say, or is it asking you to sit next to someone who shouldn't get that? What's the version that keeps your dignity?
