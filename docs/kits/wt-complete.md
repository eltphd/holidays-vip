## 1. What's inside, in the order we'd use them

1. **Coming Home** (practice). Two mirrored decks, one for the one returning and one for the ones receiving. First, before anyone travels.
2. **Open House** (make). The gathering: the welcome, the name tags, the three scripts, seven table cards. The day the most people are in the house.
3. **Elder Light** (practice). The elder's recording, letter, keep-list, and Umoja night. Across the week, pointed at Dec 26.
4. **Chosen Family Table** (make). A feast for the family you chose. The night that family is the one in the room.

## 3. The Family Soul Compass readout

**What it is.** One page the family fills in at the end of the season, together, from the check-ins the kits already carry: the both-sides check-in in Coming Home, the three lines on the Umoja card in Elder Light, the words on the back of the Open House place cards. Nothing new to collect.

A few of the prompts on the page:
- "What did we do this year that we should keep?"
- "Who was at the table this year who wasn't last year?"
- "One word for the season, each. No reasons."
- "One thing for next year."

Twenty minutes, and it goes in the drawer with the letter and the cards.

**What it is not.** It isn't a score, a survey, or something you send anywhere. Nothing individual leaves the house: not a word, not a card, not a name. It doesn't tell you how your family did. It's your family telling itself.

The aggregate season readout from Measurement Ally is a separate thing. After the season, bundle households get a separate invitation to share, aggregate only, with its own consent step. Saying no changes nothing. Nobody at holidayz.vip sees your page; the default is that nothing leaves.

**How it's delivered.** The readout page is the last two pages of this file. Print it or fill it on a phone. No login, no link, no upload. The January invitation, if you get one, comes by the same neutral email.

**Time:** 20 minutes. **Sensory:** one voice at a time, sitting, no candle required. **Low-demand:** everybody says one word. Somebody writes them down. That's the readout.
