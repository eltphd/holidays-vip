## Before you open this (read this first)

This is written to you. Not to your parents, not to whoever's hosting. If somebody handed it to you, it's yours now. If a grown-up is reading over your shoulder, that's fine, but the "you" in here is you, and there's nothing in here for them to do.

Four things are in here. A one-page sheet that says your terms for the week, which you show to somebody or don't. A menu of ways to be at a family thing without being on the whole time. A journal that nobody reads. And one night of the season that's yours to design from nothing.

These are practices, not treatment. We say where each one comes from. We don't say what it'll do for you, because we don't know you, and anyone who tells you a worksheet is going to change your December is selling something.

The house rule for the whole kit: **anyone can pass.** Every line, every prompt, every night. Passing isn't failing the kit; it's one of the answers, printed on every page so nobody has to explain it.

## 1. "My terms for the holidays" (the one-pager)

[MALIA — your voice here: the intro to the one-pager. Why someone our age would bother filling this out, and what it's actually for.]

This is one page you fill for yourself. Then you decide what happens to it: show it to one person, show it to everybody, leave it on the counter, or show it to nobody. Every one of those is the right answer. "Nobody" is a who.

Where it comes from: the second night of Kwanzaa is Kujichagulia, self-determination, which in plain terms means you say who you are and nobody says it for you. This page is that, for one week.

Time: about ten minutes. Sensory: pen and paper, or a notes app; alone or with music on. Low-demand: fill one line. "Call me" and "If I leave the room" do most of the work.

```
MY TERMS FOR THE HOLIDAYS
Fill what you want. Skip what you don't. Every line has a pass, and pass is an answer.

Call me: __________________________________________              [ ] pass

When I go quiet, it usually means: _______________________________   [ ] pass

A thing I'm fine doing this week: ________________________________   [ ] pass

A thing I'd rather sit out this year: ____________________________   [ ] pass

If I leave the room, the plan is: ________________________________   [ ] pass
  (for example: I'm back in twenty. Text me. Don't follow.)

A topic that's off the table for me: _____________________________   [ ] pass

One thing I actually want out of this week: ______________________   [ ] pass

If something's wrong, the person I'd want to hear it from: ________   [ ] pass

Sharing this is optional. I decide who sees it, and whether anyone does.
Seen by: ___________________    [ ] no one, and that's the answer
```

**How to fill it.** Short answers. A word is enough. "Call me: Jay." "If I leave: back in twenty, don't follow." It's a card you might hand over, not an essay.

**If you share it.** Hand it over, text a photo, or say two lines from it out loud at the start of the week. You can fold half of it under. You can show one cousin and nobody else.

**If somebody argues with it.** It's your terms, not an opening offer. You don't owe a reason for any line. "That one's a pass" ends it. You can also take the page back; it was never a contract.

## 2. The participation menu (in the room without being on)

[MALIA — your voice here: one or two lines on what "being on" at a family thing actually feels like.]

Being at the holiday and being on at the holiday are two different things, and most of the pressure comes from people treating them as one. The menu splits them. Pick a level per event, or per hour. Move up or down whenever; moving down in the middle of dinner is the menu working. Nobody has to know what level you're on.

Where it comes from: Ujima, the third night, collective work. The family runs on everybody doing a piece, not everybody doing everything. A piece can be small.

Time: choosing takes ten seconds. Sensory: each level says what it needs. Low-demand: level 0 or 1, all week, and it still counts.

**Level 0 — In the house.** You're here. Door open or closed, your call. That's already participating; you came.

**Level 1 — In the room, headphones on.** Couch, floor, corner chair. One earbud or two. If somebody asks what you're listening to, "music" is a complete sentence.

**Level 2 — In the room, hands busy.** Phone, a game, a sketchbook, the little cousins' puzzle, folding napkins. You're present. You aren't on stage.

**Level 3 — One job.** One task, yours: the playlist, the ice, the pickup run, the dishes with one other person, twenty minutes with the little kids. A job gives you somewhere to be and a real reason to leave any conversation.

**Level 4 — At the table.** Sitting, eating, listening. Talking if you want to. If somebody puts you on the spot, "I'll pass on that one" works out loud, at the table, and you don't have to say it twice.

**Level 5 — In it.** Games, stories, the whole thing. You picked it. You can un-pick it.

**Level 6 — Hosting one thing.** You run one piece of the day: a dish, a game, the toast, the eighth night (section 4). You're in charge of that one thing and not one thing more.

**Two lines for when somebody asks why you're not at the table.** "I'm here." Or: "Headphones for a bit. I'll be over." Neither one is a defense.

## 3. "Things I'd never say at the table" (the journal)

This part is private by design. Nobody reads it. Not your parents, not the host, not a counselor, not us. It doesn't get turned in. It isn't a check-in. It isn't part of any family readout or any report to anybody. If it's in a notes app, lock the note. If it's paper, keep it where you keep things.

[MALIA — your voice here: how you'd say "nobody reads this" so that someone our age actually believes it.]

Where it comes from: the oldest thing there is. People have kept what they couldn't say in a drawer since there were drawers.

Time: five minutes or an hour. Sensory: alone, or in the room with headphones; a locked note or a notebook. Low-demand: read the prompts and write nothing. Reading them counts.

Every prompt has a pass, and a page that says "pass" is a page. There's a tear-out line at the bottom of every journal page: if you write something and don't want it to exist, tear it off.

1. The thing I'd say at the table if I knew nobody would react. [ ] pass
2. The person here I'm most like, and the way I don't want to be like them. [ ] pass
3. What I'm actually doing when I go quiet. [ ] pass
4. Something somebody said this year that I'm still carrying. (You don't have to write who.) [ ] pass
5. What I'd want the holidays to be if I got to design them from zero. [ ] pass
6. A thing I'm good at that this family doesn't see. [ ] pass
7. Who I'd sit next to if there were no seating chart. Who I'd sit far from. [ ] pass
8. A sentence I wish somebody would say to me this week. Write it here; it counts as having been said once. [ ] pass
9. The version of me that shows up to family stuff, and the version that shows up with my friends. Which one is more tired. [ ] pass
10. One thing from this year I'm carrying forward on purpose. [ ] pass

[MALIA — your voice here: two or three prompts that are yours. Replace any of the ten above that sound like an adult wrote them.]

If something you write in here scares you when you read it back, the card at the front is there and those numbers pick up. That's the only time this kit will bring it up.

## 4. The eighth night (yours to design)

Kwanzaa is seven nights, December 26 through January 1, one principle a night. There is no eighth night, which is the point: the eighth night is the one the family invents, and in this kit it's yours to invent. January 2, or any night of the break, or after the last candle goes out, or in a house that doesn't light candles at all. If your family doesn't do Kwanzaa, you still get it; it's the one night of the season somebody your age designed.

[MALIA — your voice here: the intro to the eighth night. What you'd actually do with one night nobody else planned.]

Where it comes from: Kuumba, the sixth night, creativity: to leave the place better and more beautiful than you found it. A night nobody planned is the most creative thing on the calendar.

Time: twenty minutes to fill this in, then the night itself, as long as you say. Sensory: you set all of it; the template asks you to. Low-demand: fill in "the one thing we do" and nothing else. A night with one thing in it is a night.

```
THE EIGHTH NIGHT
Designed by: ______________   (or leave it blank; nobody has to know it was you)

When: ______________________   (a date, "after the last candle," "whenever")    [ ] pass

Where: _____________________   (living room, porch, the car, a group chat)      [ ] pass

Who's in: __________________   ("everybody," "three people," "just me")         [ ] pass

The one thing we do: ______________________________________________        [ ] pass
  (a food, a game, a movie, a walk, a playlist, a silence, a thing we make)

How long: __________________   (twenty minutes is a night)                      [ ] pass

Sound: [ ] quiet   [ ] normal   [ ] loud   [ ] I'll set it live

What nobody has to do: ____________________________________________
  (talk, perform, explain, stay till the end)

A word for it, if you want one: ______________
  (your own; it doesn't have to be one of the seven)

How it ends: _______________   ("when I say," "when the food's gone," "no ending")  [ ] pass
```

**Handing it over.** Give your people the whole page, or one line ("Wednesday, porch, eight, bring nothing"), or nothing, and do it alone. If they say no this year, you still designed a night; keep the page for next year. There's no correct eighth night. Four people eating cereal on the floor through one movie is a complete one if that's what you wrote down.

## The bare minimum (low-demand version)

If you have five minutes and no energy, do these three and nothing else:

1. Fill one line on the one-pager. "Call me" or "If I leave the room." Show it to nobody, or one person.
2. Pick a level off the menu and don't announce it.
3. Read the journal prompts. Write nothing.

That's the kit. Everything else in here is extra.
