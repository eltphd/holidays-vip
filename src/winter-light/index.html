What "culturally competent" means here

Not décor. Design.

Most holiday products for Black and brown families are either generic with a color swap or a box of symbols with no practice inside. We build from the other direction: cultural memory as intellectual infrastructure, emotional literacy as a family skill, and a schedule your neurodivergent kid can actually predict.

Every package has to pass six standards before it ships. They are the product.

  1. 01

    Safety is the first feature

    Vetted hosts and places, a published safeguarding plan, background-checked staff, adult-to-teen ratios stated on the page you buy from.

  2. 02

    Representation in every touchpoint

    Black and brown facilitators, hosts, artists, and makers. The families in the photos are real families using the kits.

  3. 03

    Heritage as content, not costume

    Each kit and journey carries a real historical, artistic, or scientific thread — the Nguzo Saba as a family operating system; Afro–Costa Rican Limón as a living archive.

  4. 04

    Teen agency, peer register

    Teens co-design one element of every experience. Creative labs and circles, never sessions. Nothing that feels like school.

  5. 05

    Neurodivergent-first

    Schedules published in advance, sensory-option rooms, opt-out norms, low-demand mornings, no surprise activities.

  6. 06

    Measured — and the data comes back to you

    Before-and-after emotional-literacy and belonging measures. Your family gets its own Soul Compass readout. Institutions get an impact report.

The safety promise

Seventy-nine percent of Black travelers rank safety first. So do we.

Every experience publishes its safeguarding plan before you pay: who is on staff, how they were vetted, the adult-to-teen ratio, the emergency plan, and what a teen can opt out of. Youth travel is designed to the ISO 31031 standard. International journeys are fulfilled with licensed operators and require travel-medical coverage.

Measured, not marketed

Your family gets the data back.

Before and after each season, families complete short emotional-literacy and belonging measures. You receive your own Soul Compass readout — a plain, private picture of what shifted. Organizations receive aggregate impact reports. Nothing is sold, shared, or scored against you.

Twenty years: classroom, program, research, evaluation

"The embargo on brilliance does not pause for the holidays. It shows up as the kit that is socks and tea, and the trip where your teen is tolerated instead of designed for. This is the reversal."

I have spent twenty years studying one question from four vantage points — the classroom, the program, the dissertation, and evaluation practice: what keeps young people's brilliance intact, and what quietly embargoes it. holidayz.vip is that research, made into something a family can hold in December. My own teenager, a neurodivergent learner, is the design lens for every schedule, prompt, and room.
